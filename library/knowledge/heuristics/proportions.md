These are starting points, not rules. Break them on purpose when the design calls for it.

## Storey height
- **Try 4–5 blocks floor to floor** for houses (3–4 blocks of clear interior plus the floor). Why: 3 blocks of clear space feels cramped once a player stands inside, and 6+ makes small rooms look like halls.
- **Raise the ground storey by one block** over the upper ones in shops, halls and town houses. Why: a taller base reads as "public" and gives the facade a visual footing.
- **Civic and sacred buildings can go 7–12 blocks per storey.** Height is what signals importance at a glance.

## Footprint
- **Avoid perfect squares for houses; try roughly 2:3 or 3:5** (e.g. 7×11, 9×15). Why: a long axis gives the roof a ridge direction and the building a front.
- **Odd widths (5, 7, 9, 11) centre a door, window or ridge on one block.** Even widths need a 2-wide centre feature or an off-centre composition.
- **Break footprints over ~15 blocks** with a wing, porch or step back. Why: one big box reads as a warehouse at Minecraft scale.

## Height vs. width
- Facades look balanced when **wall height is about 0.5–1× the facade width** before the roof. Much lower looks like a shed; much higher looks like a tower (fine, if a tower is the point).
- Towers read as towers from about **2.5× taller than wide**.

## Scale cues
- Doors (2 high), fences and slabs give scale. Size other elements against the door: a window head usually lines up with the door head.
- Big builds need **bigger details** (2–3 block thick trim, deeper recesses), or they look flat from a distance.
