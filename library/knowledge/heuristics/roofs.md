Suggestions to weigh. The roof is often the largest visible surface, so it carries a lot of the style.

## Pitch with blocks
| Step pattern (rise:run) | Built with | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 1:2 | slabs stepping half a block | low, Mediterranean, modern |
| 1:1 | stairs, one up per one across | the default; neutral |
| 2:1 | stairs plus full blocks, two up per one across | steep: northern, gothic, timber-frame |

- **1:1 is safe but common.** Why: it's what most builds use, so it reads as "default". A steeper or shallower pitch is a cheap way to give a build character.
- **Steep roofs suit narrow buildings** (≤9 wide). On wide footprints they get very tall; consider a hip or a lower pitch.
- **Mix pitches with care:** one pitch per building (dormers excepted) keeps it calm.

## Overhang
- **Give eaves 1 block of overhang**, with upside-down stairs or slabs underneath. Why: the shadow line under the eave separates wall from roof and hides the blocky join.
- Gable ends can overhang too; a 1-block push-out with a trim stair along the verge reads well.

## Shape
- **Gable:** simplest; shows a strong front. Put the gable toward the street for town houses, side-on for cottages.
- **Hip:** calmer, good for wide or square plans and for corners.
- **Combine** a main roof and a lower cross roof for an L or T plan instead of one big roof over everything.

## Material
- Use a roof role with **stairs and slabs available** (palette sheets show this as shape pips). A family without stairs can still fill the gable triangle or the ridge.
- A darker roof than the walls makes the building look grounded; a lighter roof makes it look airy.
